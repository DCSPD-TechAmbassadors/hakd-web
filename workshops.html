Workshops

Workshop Listing

Kick off the hackathon by diving into two workshops of your choice on Day 1. You’ll be choosing from 10 different hands-on sessions led by experienced volunteers and experts. Whether you'll be learning new tools, exploring new software, or refining your own technical skills, these workshops are designed to give you practical knowledge you can directly apply to your projects. Come curious, stay engaged, and use this opportunity wisely!

Robot Wars

Run by: Elaine Wu

Description: Introduction of Robot wars (fighting robots) by using ppts and videos to let students know what exactly it is, and then a little tournament.

Max capacity: 16

Requirements: None

Rocket Building - an intro to projectile motion

Run by: Jasmine Eklund, Terence Ng, Alan Yang, Max Zhang, Baron Song, Arnav Chhaparwal, Tanush Guha

Description: Students will be introduced to the physics of projectile motion through kinematic equations and get the chance to build their very own rocket using plastic bottles, which they can then launch and discover how the rockets shape affects it's aerodynamics and how the addition of water inside the rocket being expelled as it's launched can change its trajectory.

Max capacity: 15

Requirements: None

Arduino Sensor and Servo Lab: Measure, Move, and Create!

Run by: Ethan Liu, Jessica Yang

Description: This hands-on workshop introduces participants to ultrasonic sensors and servo motors. Participants will learn how to measure distance using an ultrasonic sensor and control a servo motor to create interactive projects.

Recommended skill level: intermediate

Max capacity: 15

Requirements: Arduino IDE

Raspberry Pi Pico and Sensing

Run by: Chase Yiu

Description: What's better than making things move? Making them sense the world around us! Explore the fascinating world of Raspberry Pi Pico, one of the most versatile and powerful microcontrollers (so far). Get ready to unleash your creativity and programming skills to make them sense distance, brightness, rotation, or even program them to make your own joystick!

Some prior knowledge on Python and digital electronics (simply, how electricity works) is recommended.

Max capacity: 12

Requirements: Thonny IDE

Hands-On Bioelectricity: A Beginner’s Guide to EMG and Brain-Computer Interfaces

Only available for Year 9 (Grade 8) and above

Run by: Yuting Zheng, Calvin Liao

Description: Have you ever imagined controlling a computer directly with your mind? Or what about a third robotic arm that moves as naturally as your own? In this hands-on workshop, we’ll explore the magic of bioelectricity. Use EMG sensors and Arduino to see the electric signals of your muscle movement in real-time, and even try controlling devices with your mind (well , almost!).

Max capacity: 20

Requirements: Arduino IDE

Prototyping in Minecraft

Only available for Year 8 (Grade 7) and below

Run by: Dean An

Description: Students will try out their ideas, either build a settlement or a prototype of a device, or anything exciting, in the virtual world of Minecraft.

Max capacity: 20

Requirements: Minecraft Education

Training and using machine learning models

Run by: Matt Chermside

Description: Experience training some machine learning models to recognise patterns in data collected through sensors, and then evaluate their effectiveness.

Max capacity: 15

Requirements: None

Dash the Robot

Run by: Priyanka (Priya) Soni

Description: Discover the wonderful world of the Wonder Workshop with Dash! Dash is a playful robot designed for easy access to the world of coding and robotics. Dash uses Bluetooth, 3 microphones, speakers, 3 processors, sensor fusion and LED lights to create a super fun user experience. In this interactive workshop, you will explore a variety of apps and move through a host of challenges with Dash. Come join the fun!

Max capacity: 12

Requirements: Go, Wonder, Blockly

Come and have a slice of Raspberry Pi

Run by: Phil Whitaker

Description: In this workshop you'll be introduced to the Raspberry Pi. This is aimed at Raspberry Pi beginners (or those in need of a refresher). You'll be provided with everything you need, just bring a can-do attitude. This workshop is suitable for those who may already have some Arduino (or other) experience, and want to learn how a more advanced board works - i.e. a board that has an OS and can be used with Python. You'll also use a breadboard and some simple I/O components.

Max capacity: 10

Requirements: None

Life cycle of a rocket

Run by: Xiaqing(Samuel)Liu

Description: Imagine all the cool things you can explore by building a rocket-launch it, enter orbits, explore space, aim for the stars-but hey! Do you really know what exactly you're doing? Or, rather, do you know what you should do for any project, for anything? Simply pursuing your dream and dreaming a million dreams but not doing any of them certainly is not the solution. Join this workshop to find out.

Max capacity: 20

Requirements: None
